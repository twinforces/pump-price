# Renewable diesel

Locked 2026-09-27. Some of the plants that "shut" did not go dark. They stopped running crude. They started running fat.

## Not biodiesel

Biodiesel is an ester, made by reacting fat or oil with methanol. It is a different molecule from diesel. Engines and pipelines only want a little of it, often 5 or 20 percent.

Renewable diesel is a hydrocarbon. Carbon and hydrogen, same family as the diesel that comes from crude. The usual process is hydrotreating: fat or oil plus hydrogen, under heat and pressure, over a catalyst. That is the same kind of unit a refinery already uses to pull sulfur out of petroleum diesel, which is why a crude plant can be converted without being torn down. The still that boiled crude is the part that stops. The hydrotreater keeps going, and it needs more hydrogen for fat than it needed for sulfur.

The fuel meets the petroleum-diesel specification (ASTM D975). It can ride in a petroleum pipeline. It can be sold alone or blended in any amount. A truck cannot tell the two apart without a carbon-dating test.

The feedstock is fat and oil. Soybean oil, canola, used cooking oil, animal fat. Not one of the four crude buckets.

## Why a refinery would do this

The Department of Energy says nearly all renewable diesel made or imported here is used in California, because California's low-carbon fuel standard pays for it. Federal renewable-fuel credits pay as well. A barrel of fat, plus those credits, paid some owners more than a barrel of crude. The plant did not close because the stills fell apart. It stopped buying crude because the other barrel paid better.

The hydrogen is the catch in the name. Making it usually starts from natural gas. The Energy Information Administration notes that the hydrogen source can decide whether the fuel qualifies for the credits. "Renewable" describes the fat, not the whole process.

## How big

As of January 1, 2026, the federal capacity tables show 19 plants that can make renewable diesel and the other biofuels that are not ethanol and not biodiesel: renewable jet, renewable naphtha, and the like. By fuel district the capacities add to 324 thousand barrels a day. The Gulf Coast has 160 of that. The West Coast has 119. The East Coast has none.

Actual renewable diesel production, not the nameplate, was 258 thousand barrels a day in June 2026. It was about 50 thousand barrels a day at the start of 2021, and about 190 thousand as an average for 2025. Distillate, diesel plus heating oil, is 3.9 million barrels a day. So this is on the order of 7 percent of distillate. Large enough to matter in the diesel balance. Not a new crude supply.

The same hydrotreater can make renewable jet instead. Fat that becomes jet is fat that did not become diesel. That is the yield fight again, with a feedstock that is also food.

## The lever

A converted plant is not the refinery lever set to off.

Crude in: off. Gasoline out: off. That part of the closure is real, and it is a gasoline lever.

Diesel out: not off. A different diesel comes out, from fat, capped by how much fat exists and by whether the credits are paying. Those gallons count in the diesel pump. They do not count in the crude pie, and they do not refill the gasoline the plant stopped making.

If the credits go away, the lever flips back only if the still is still there. Many of these conversions scrapped the crude unit. Off stays off.
