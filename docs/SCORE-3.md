# Gulf score

Locked 2026-09-27. The national leftover was the wrong object. A station's own cut is tight. The pump also falls slower than the harbor, and a fixed delay does not fix the score.

## What changed

The earlier 44 cents on gasoline was the Gulf Coast harbor against the national pump. That gap includes California and the Northeast. It is not what a dealer keeps.

This score stays on the Gulf Coast. Harbor price, Gulf Coast spot. Pump, Gulf Coast retail. Tax, the federal rate plus Texas's 20 cents a gallon, the state statute since 1991, the same for diesel. Texas has no local motor-fuel tax. The other Gulf states are not all at 20 cents, so a piece of the leftover is still their tax.

The leftover, pump minus harbor minus that tax, median of January 2015 through June 16, 2025:

| | Held still |
| --- | --- |
| Gasoline | 22 cents |
| Diesel | 54 cents |

Twenty-two cents is the size of a truck, a blend, and a tight dealer, taken together. It is not the dealer's profit. Fifty-four cents on diesel is too wide to call a station margin. That split is not done.

## The slow fall

A harbor price from two weeks earlier, or four, does not lift the typical week. On gasoline it trims the spring 2020 miss from about 55 percent low to about 47 percent low. The delay is real. It is not a constant clock, and it is not the 1 percent.
