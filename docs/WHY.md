# Why those prices

Locked 2026-09-27. The question was why gasoline rose around one inauguration, eased around the next, and why diesel is so high now. The answers are levers we already have, or one we add. They are not a term for who was president.

Regular gasoline is the unleaded regular column of the Energy Information Administration's monthly price table, taxes included. Diesel is the on-highway series. The June 2022 peak is the weekly survey.

Diesel, week of September 14, 2026: $6.29 a gallon. Highest nominal print since this series began in 1994. On an inflation-adjusted basis, the highest since 2022. That is the Energy Information Administration's own sentence.

## Gasoline up in the first window

Three levers, in order.

Demand came back. The 2020 average was the COVID collapse. The 2021 average is people driving again. That started before the inauguration. December 2020 and the 2020 average are the same $2.17.

Plants had closed. From the start of 2020 to the start of 2022, operable stills fell by about one million barrels a day, from 19.0 million to 17.9 million. Pandemic losses, and some plants converted to renewable diesel. A closed plant is the refinery lever, set to off. It does not come back because a term changed.

A country left the market. The weekly peak is June 2022, sixteen months after the inauguration, after Russia invaded Ukraine. That is the country-supply lever. A release from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve pushed the other way in the same year. It is a temporary supply add, not a new kind of lever.

The federal gasoline tax did not change. It is still 18.4 cents.

## Gasoline down in the next window

Most of the decline happened before that inauguration. From $5.01 in June 2022 to $3.21 in January 2025 is $1.80, and it is two and a half years of the previous chapter. From January 2025 ($3.21) to January 2026 ($2.96) is 25 cents. February 2026 was $3.07. That is the whole pre-strait move.

We do not have a lever that flipped in January 2025 and explains even that quarter. Crude and demand had already eased. Naming a policy we have not measured would be the thing the validation rule forbids. The strait then reversed it: February $3.07 to March $3.99.

## Why diesel is the expensive one now

Same crude goes into both fuels. The gap is the crack, the refining margin on diesel.

The Energy Information Administration, September 17, 2026: distillate is tight because refineries in Russia, China, and the Middle East are producing less of it. Imports cost more. The United States is exporting more, because the world price is higher than the home price. Stocks are low. The September outlook expects diesel crack spreads above $2 a gallon from August through November 2026, and stocks under 100 million barrels. A barrel is 42 gallons, so $2 a gallon of crack is $84 a barrel of margin, on top of the crude.

In June 2026, while a truce was being talked about, crude futures fell about 22 percent on the month and diesel futures fell about 9 percent. The product did not follow the crude. Reuters reported the diesel crack at $62.84 a barrel on June 25, with distillate stocks at 106 million barrels, about 12 million under the five-year average.

Jet fuel is the same cut of the barrel as diesel. When jet pays more, the plant makes jet. That is the yield lever, not a new fuel.

So the diesel lever is not "diesel is special now." It is: middle-distillate supply off (those regions), export pull on, inventories thin, and the plant choosing jet versus diesel. The Semi is none of these. That lever is withdrawn.
