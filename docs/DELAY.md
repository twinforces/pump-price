# The delays

Locked 2026-09-27. The clocks are published. They were not searched. Transit moves barrels. It does not lag the quoted price.

The harbor in the Gulf score is already finished gasoline and diesel. The refinery is behind that price.

| Step | How long | What the price did |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A ship of crude, the Persian Gulf to the US Gulf the long way | 40 to 45 days | Not a lag on the whole price. Only the barrels on that water. The futures move the day of the news. |
| Crude already at the refinery, turned into fuel | about 3 days | Shorter than a weekly score can see. No separate term. |
| Finished fuel, Gulf Coast to New York on the Colonial pipeline | gasoline about 15 days, diesel about 19, at about 5 miles an hour | The price does not wait. |
| The pile at the refinery and the terminal | about 25 days of gasoline sales, about 33 of diesel | Already in the score. This one does move the sign, because it is inventory cost, not a pipe. |
| The station's own tank | several days at a corner station, about a day at a busy truck stop | Written down. Not added on top of the pile. |

## The pipe, run

January 2015 through June 16, 2025, COVID weeks not in this comparison of harbors. New York harbor minus the Gulf, 545 weeks.

| | Same week | Waiting for the pipe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gasoline, typical gap | 6 cents | 10 cents, at 2 weeks |
| Diesel, typical gap | 5 cents | 11 cents, at 3 weeks |

Waiting makes the match worse. The East Coast pump says the same thing. Against the Gulf spot, a typical East Coast gasoline week is within 3.0 percent either way, and the two-week wait does not tighten it. East Coast diesel is the loose one: a typical week within 4.7 percent of the Gulf spot, and 4.4 percent of the New York harbor, with dozens of weeks outside 10 percent. The pipe is not why.

The ship is the same rule. A closure shows up in the futures the day it happens. The barrels already at sea keep arriving for 40 to 45 days. That arrival is a volume. It is not a reason to lag the quoted price.
