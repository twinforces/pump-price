# The delays

Locked 2026-09-27, corrected the same day. The quote is imaginary. The transit is real. Gasoline and diesel share a movement. They are not two oceans.

## The quote

New York harbor minus the Gulf, 545 weeks, January 2015 through June 16, 2025.

| | Same week | Waiting for the pipe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gasoline | 6 cents | 10 cents, at 2 weeks |
| Diesel | 5 cents | 11 cents, at 3 weeks |

The quotes move together. That does not mean the gallon has arrived. Waiting for the pipe makes the quote worse, so the quote is not a delayed gallon.

## The gallon

One departure, both fuels.

| Movement | Gasoline | Diesel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The pipeline, one line, batches taking turns | about 15 days | about 19 days |
| A product tanker, one hull | about 8 days on the water | the same day, if it is aboard |
| A crude ship, the long way, then the plant | 40 to 45 days, then about 3 | the same cargo becomes both |

The tanker is not free for another load until the round trip is done, about 20 days. Not every hull carries both grades. When it does, they dock together.

The pile, about 25 days of gasoline sales and 33 of diesel, is still the inventory cost in the score. It is not a substitute for the hull or the batch.
