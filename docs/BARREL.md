# The barrel

Locked 2026-09-27. A market exists for every cut that leaves the plant. The street map still shows gasoline and diesel. The refinery view has to show the rest, or the plant's choice is fake.

Spelling is naphtha. One p, then h.

## What a United States refinery actually makes

A 42-gallon barrel goes in. Cracking makes more gallons than it takes. In 2023 the average was about 45 gallons of products out, a processing gain of about 6 percent.

## Naphtha

Naphtha is the light cut above gasoline's heavier blendstocks and below the gases. Inside the refinery most of it is upgraded (reformed, alkylated) and leaves as gasoline. That is why the sold-as-naphtha line is about 1 percent, not a quarter of the barrel.

The unsold-to-gasoline remainder has a market. Chemical plants crack it into plastics. Blenders buy it when it is worth more in gasoline than in a chemical plant. Platts assesses it. The Asian cargo price is the benchmark the chemical trade watches. We do not have a free series in the repo yet. The pie may show the gallons. It may not invent the price.

Heavy crude makes less of this light cut and more residual, coke, and diesel. Light sweet makes more naphtha and gasoline. That is why the four crude slices are not cosmetic.

## California

There is not one additive that explains California's pump price.

The certified additive is real. Since 1990, gasoline sold for the road in California has had to contain a deposit-control additive certified by the Air Resources Board, at or above the dosage in that certification. The tests cover fuel injectors, intake valves, and combustion chambers. The federal government also requires a detergent. California's certificate is its own, and it is stricter. https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/our-work/programs/gasoline/gasoline-program-requirements

Ethanol is not the statewide year-round mandate people think it is. The Air Resources Board's own answers say the reformulated-gasoline rules do not require ethanol. A winter oxygen minimum (1.8 percent by weight, November through February) applies in the South Coast and Imperial County. Elsewhere, oxygen from zero up to 10 percent ethanol by volume is allowed. In practice most California gallons are 10 percent ethanol. Ethanol is the only oxygenate still allowed. The ether MTBE has been banned since the end of 2003. The unfinished blendstock that becomes legal California gasoline only after the oxygenate goes in is called CARBOB. https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/resources/fact-sheets/gasoline-frequently-asked-questions

The expensive part is the recipe, not the teaspoon of detergent: sulfur, benzene, aromatics, olefins, vapor pressure, and the predictive model a refiner has to pass. Few plants can make that blendstock. That is the green-fuel lever for California. Oregon stays on that lever until we write its own rule. It is not this additive.

## What the screen owes the barrel

A yield stack next to the crude pie. Gasoline and diesel on the map. Jet, residual, coke, asphalt, and sold naphtha on the stack, each marked with whether we have a price series or only the gallons. Still gas is labeled as burned on site, not as a sale.

No coefficient that says "the California additive is 80 cents." The spec is a constraint on who can make the fuel. The price is whatever clears.
