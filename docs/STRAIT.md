# The strait was not a switch

Locked 2026-09-27. March 2026 took a lot of Hormuz oil off the water. It did not take the Saudi east-west line out, and it did not take the strait to zero. The published world balance is not a ship count. No barrels from this page are turned into cents.

Iran's wells do not have to stop if the exports stop. The country already burns about half its crude at home. That offtake is enough to keep wells turning. They would slow to what the refineries and the tanks can take. That is not a collapse, and it is not a permanent half.

The east-west line runs from the Saudi fields to Yanbu on the Red Sea, so those barrels never enter the strait. Its nameplate is about 7 million barrels a day. About 2 million of that feeds refineries on the west coast, so the export room is smaller than the nameplate. In March the line was up. Accounts from that month put Yanbu crude loadings around 4 to 5 million barrels a day, against well under 1 million before the war. The docks, not the pipe, were the tight piece. The line was hit later in the year and restarted. March is not that later hit.

What still came through the strait is the fog. One ship tracker put crude that had to pass the strait near 20 million barrels a day at the start of March and under 3 million in the week of March 15. Another account of a normal year is nearer 15 million through the strait. The outlook we charted shows world supply falling from about 106 million barrels a day in January to about 97 in March and 95 in April. A weekly loading collapse, a monthly production estimate, and a bypass that stayed open are three different objects. Traders are trying to turn the difference into money. We will not pick one of them and call it the supply.
