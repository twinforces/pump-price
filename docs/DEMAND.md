# Demand baseline

Locked 2026-09-27. One hundred percent is 2019, the same week or the same month. It is not a round number and it is not one fuel.

## What is being measured

The Energy Information Administration does not survey every tank. Product supplied is the proxy: production plus imports, minus exports, minus the change in primary stocks. It is the volume that left the primary system. The agency says this is not the same thing as a purchase at the pump. It is the series we have.

Finished motor gasoline and distillate fuel oil are separate series. They do not share a percent.

## Why 2019

It is the last full year before driving collapsed, and gasoline was already on a plateau.

| Year | Gasoline, thousand barrels a day | Distillate, thousand barrels a day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2018 | 9,329 | 4,146 |
| 2019 | 9,309 | 4,103 |
| 2020 | 8,049 | 3,786 |
| 2025 | 8,935 | 3,908 |

Gasoline in 2020 was 87 percent of the 2019 year. Distillate was 92 percent. Freight did not stop the way commuting did. One shared percent would hide that. The single demand percent already in the code is the wrong shape. It splits.

A later year cannot be the baseline. Using 2023 or 2025 would define the recovery as normal and make 2020 the only odd year by construction.

## Same week, not the annual average

January is always quieter than July. If 100 percent were the 2019 annual average, a normal January would look like a demand crash. So the historical percent for a week is that week's product supplied divided by the 2019 week with the same week-of-year. A month uses that month of 2019.

The player lever is the same idea. Eighty percent means four fifths of the 2019 rate for that week, not four fifths of 9.3 million.

The annual figures above are the scale, so a percent has a size. They are not the denominator.
