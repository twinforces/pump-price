# Station margin

Locked 2026-09-27. The dealer's profit is about a dime. The fear is an episode, not a pad.

## The gross and the net

The National Association of Convenience Stores, using OPIS, puts the 2025 gross markup at 39.7 cents a gallon, 12.7 percent of a $3.12 average price. The five years before that averaged 38.3 cents. Expenses to sell the gallon run about 20 to 25 cents. Card fees were 8.4 cents in 2024.

A 2020 itemized cut, from the same association, started at a 35 cent gross and landed near 13 cents net: 6 cents for the truck from the terminal, 8.4 for the cards, 6 for the store, 2 for the building, 1 for the inventory swing.

The 40 cents is not the profit. The dime is.

## Why our leftover is 22 cents, not 40

The Gulf score's 22 cents is a ten-year median of the Gulf pump minus the Gulf harbor minus the federal tax minus Texas's 20 cents. The national 40 cents is a recent gross, pump minus the local rack. The rack has already paid to move the gallon. Margins also widened after 2020. They are not the same object, and they are not the dealer's take.

Diesel's 54 cent Gulf leftover is still too wide to call a station margin.

## The fear

Replacement cost. If the harbor rises and the sign does not, the next truckload costs more than the gallons just sold, so the sign rises fast. If the harbor falls and the sign drops the same day, the expensive gallons already in the ground are sold at a loss, so the sign waits. On the way up the margin often goes negative. The slow fall is where it is earned back.

Measured on the Gulf, not assumed. The usual leftover is 22 cents. The week of March 30, 2020 the gap was 96 cents: harbor 42 cents, pump $1.76, an extra 74 cents. From March through June the extra averaged 26 cents. Eight of those eighteen weeks were more than 20 cents fatter than usual.

That 74 cents is not added every week. A two-week or four-week clock does not remove it.
