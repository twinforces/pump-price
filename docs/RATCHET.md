# The ratchet

Locked 2026-09-27. A hypothesis, not a fitted coefficient. The dime and the fifteen cents are the proposed band. They were not searched against the pump.

## The band

A station's profit is about a dime. A ten-dollar move in the barrel is 24 cents a gallon, because a barrel is 42 gallons. The dime cannot absorb it, so the sign rises with the harbor.

It does not fall for a dip inside the profit. The proposed rule is a ratchet. The sign goes up with the rise. It comes down only when the drop is large enough to force it, on the order of 15 cents: past the dime, and then some. A 10 cent dip is ignored. A 15 cent gap against the town, or against the gallons already paid for, is the shove.

The reluctance is not a fixed 15 cents forever. After a large rise, the unwillingness to cut is in proportion to that rise. A station that just raised a lot waits longer, and needs a deeper fall, than a station that raised a nickel.

## The tank

The earliest a cut can happen is when the expensive gallons are gone. Days of cover is the tank divided by daily sales. A busy station turns in a few days and tracks the town. A quieter station still has the old price in the ground, so its sign lags. That is sales versus tank, not a different fear.

Mather's Market lagging the rest of town is this clock, if its sales are slower than its tank. We do not have that store's gallons or its daily sales, so it does not get its own number.

## March 2020

Sales collapsed and the tank stopped turning. The Gulf gap that week was 96 cents against a usual leftover of 22. Those weeks stay on the chart. They are not used to set the dime or the 15 cents. The band is estimated on the weeks when the tank was still turning.
