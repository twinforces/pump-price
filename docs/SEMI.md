# The Tesla Semi

Locked 2026-09-27. The lever is allowed. The history is not allowed to pretend the lever already moved the national diesel price.

## What is actually on the road

PepsiCo has been running Semis since late 2022. That fleet is dozens of trucks, in regional service, not a national fleet. A dedicated factory in Sparks, Nevada, is built for 50,000 trucks a year. Tesla has not published how many it is building. Outside guesses for 2026 deliveries run from 5,000 to 15,000. Those guesses are not a census. A headline that says "1,000 a week" is the factory's capacity divided by the weeks in a year, unless Tesla prints a count.

As of late 2025, electric trucks on the road in this country were mostly cargo vans. Diesel still fueled more than 95 percent of the heavy ones (the big classes, 7 and 8). A few thousand electric heavy trucks against that is a rounding error in a national price.

## The arithmetic

United States distillate, which is diesel plus heating oil, was 3.9 million barrels a day in 2025. Not all of that is highway trucks. The highway share is a number we still have to take from the federal tables before the lever is allowed to speak in cents.

The displacement formula, and it is a formula, not a result:

Gallons a year = miles a year divided by miles per gallon.

Barrels a day = that gallon count divided by 42, then by 365.

The North American Council for Freight Efficiency puts the national diesel-truck average near 7 miles per gallon. Newer trucks in their 2026 road test did better. If one truck runs 100,000 miles a year at 7 miles per gallon, that is about 14,300 gallons, about one barrel a day. The 100,000 miles is a placeholder. It gets written down, or the lever does not move a price.

| Trucks, each about one barrel a day | Distillate removed | Against 3.9 million barrels a day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 (a Pepsi-sized fleet) | about 50 barrels a day | Invisible. |
| 10,000 | about 10,000 barrels a day | About a quarter of one percent of all distillate. |
| 50,000 (a full year of the factory, if it builds that many, and if each truck replaces a diesel mile) | about 50,000 barrels a day | About 1.3 percent of all distillate. Maybe visible. Not a new price regime. |

A Semi does not delete the gallon unless it replaces a diesel mile. A truck parked at a charger, or a diesel truck that keeps running beside it, removes nothing.

## What the model may do

A god-mode slider: electric heavy-truck miles. It subtracts barrels a day from diesel demand. It does not subtract them from gasoline. It does not get its own term in the COVID chapter, the 2021-2025 chapter, or the 2025 chapter up to the strait. If the fit needs a large Semi term to match those years, the fit is wrong.

The same mile is a gallon of diesel not burned and a kilowatt-hour that is. The map has no electricity price yet. Until it does, the lever says "diesel demand down" and does not say "the economy got cheaper."

## What would change this

A published delivery count. A federal table for the highway share of distillate. A locked miles-per-year. Any one of those replaces the placeholder. None of them is a license to redraw 2020 through February 2026.
