# Two kinds of tank

Locked 2026-09-27. The corner station and the truck stop do not hold fuel for the same number of days. Neither number was fitted to the pump.

A typical fueling location sells about 2,500 gallons a day. The underground tank for a grade is on the order of 10,000 to 20,000 gallons, and stations are sized so a delivery comes every five to seven days. A quiet one goes longer. That is the consumer tank, and it is a reason a place like Mather's can lag the town.

A busy truck stop moves about 27,000 gallons of diesel a day. A corner store that sells diesel at all moves on the order of 200. A truck takes 100 to 200 gallons. A car takes 12 to 15. The truck stop's own tank turns in about a day, even when the tank is large. The long diesel cover in the score, about 33 days, is the terminal and the refinery, not the truck stop.

## The one week

October 31, 2022. The Gulf harbor jumped 26 cents for one Friday and the Gulf pump fell 4 cents. The guess was 11 percent above the sign.

A station that never took a delivery that Friday did not lose money. It was still selling what it had bought cheaper, and it did not raise. A station that took a load at the spike and sold it at the old sign gave away more than the dime. The spike was gone the next week. We cannot see who took a delivery. The average sign did not rise, so the average station most likely never bought that Friday.
