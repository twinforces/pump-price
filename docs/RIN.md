# The diesel credit

Locked 2026-09-27. The biomass-based diesel credit is real. It is not why diesel's leftover is wider than gasoline's.

The obligation is a percentage of gasoline and diesel together. The same bundle of credits is owed on a gallon of either fuel. The categories nest: the diesel credit sits inside the advanced credit, and the advanced credit sits inside the total. Adding the published percentages on top of each other would count the same gallon twice.

| Year | Diesel credit | Advanced | Total, gasoline and diesel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2015 | 1.49% | 1.62% | 9.52% |
| 2018 | 1.74% | 2.37% | 10.67% |
| 2021 | 2.16% | 3.00% | 11.19% |
| 2025 | 3.15% | 4.31% | 13.13% |

If every credit costs a dollar, 2018 is 10.7 cents on a gallon of gasoline and 10.7 cents on a gallon of diesel. If every credit costs two dollars in 2025, the bundle is about 26 cents, again on both. Giving the diesel credit to diesel alone, at the 2025 percentage and a two-dollar credit, is 6 cents. That is the largest wrong-way reading in the years we score. The leftover gap between the two fuels is about 30 cents. Six cents is not thirty.

The weekly price file is behind the agency's chart. It is not a plain download, so this is not a week-by-week series, and it is not subtracted from the score. For long stretches the diesel credit and the ethanol credit trade at nearly the same price, which makes the gap between the two fuels even smaller.

The other side of the same credit is the producer's. A gallon of biodiesel generates one and a half credits, and a gallon of renewable diesel about one and six-tenths to one and seven-tenths. At two dollars a credit, that is more than three dollars to the plant. That is revenue on the renewable gallon. It is not an adder on the petroleum pump.
