# Credits

Locked 2026-09-27. The renewable plant is a small slice of diesel. The credit is a cost on the petroleum refiner's books, and revenue on the renewable plant's books. Same credit, two balance sheets.

## What a credit is

A renewable identification number is a serial number on a gallon of renewable fuel. The Environmental Protection Agency requires refiners and importers of gasoline and diesel to turn in a set share of these numbers each year. A renewable plant creates them. A petroleum plant that does not create enough must buy them. The price is public. The agency posts a weekly, volume-weighted average from the actual trades.

California's low-carbon credit is a second market, only for fuel sold in that state. The air board posts every week's transfers: price, volume, and the average. A week in July 2026 cleared around $73 a metric ton.

The federal production tax credit that replaced the old dollar-a-gallon blender credit is not that kind of market. It is a formula on the tax return. It does not have a weekly trade print.

## Where it shows up

On the obligated refiner, the shortage is a liability. It is priced at the spot number at the end of the period, and the change runs through cost of sales. Some companies show the liability on its own line. Some net the numbers they already hold against the numbers they owe. Some bury the purchase in the cost of the fuel. The income-statement hit is the reliable one. The balance-sheet line depends on the policy.

Valero publishes the market cost of the whole obligation, not its own net bill, because it also makes renewable fuel and so generates part of what it owes. That market cost was $5.85 a barrel in 2025, against $3.75 in 2024. A barrel is 42 gallons, so $5.85 is about 14 cents a gallon, if the whole market cost were passed through. It is already inside a refining margin. It is not a second adder on top of a margin that already paid for the credits.

On the renewable plant the same number is revenue. A biomass-based diesel credit near $2.37, and more than one credit per gallon of renewable diesel, is on the order of $4 a gallon of revenue to that plant. That is why the plant runs. It is not $4 on every diesel gallon in the country.

## What the model does with it

The renewable barrels stay capped at what was made. They are a few percent of distillate, not the diesel price.

The credit that can move the pump is the obligation spread across ordinary gasoline and diesel. The series is the weekly federal credit price, and the published market cost per barrel when a refiner prints one. Fourteen cents is real. It is not the crack, and it is not the crude.

California's low-carbon credit is a state cost and a state revenue. It does not get painted onto Wyoming.
