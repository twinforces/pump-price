# Clocks and the gap

Locked 2026-09-27. A barrel on the water and a price on the screen do not move together. Storage is the quiet gap. The rest of the gap is the bet. Neither one is tuned to the pump.

## Two clocks

The paper clock is one day. The future reprices when the news hits, on every coast at once.

The ship clock is the voyage. A full very large crude carrier does not fit through the Suez Canal, so the long haul from the Persian Gulf goes around Africa.

| Arrival | Clock | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United States Gulf, the Louisiana offshore port | About 40 to 45 days. The published range for that route, depending on speed, is about 32 to 53 days. | The TD1 route, Persian Gulf to the United States Gulf. |
| Long Beach | On the order of one to two months. A ship that loaded in Iraq on February 24, 2026 was still unloading at Long Beach in early May. | The voyage, plus the Los Angeles Times account of it. |
| United States East Coast | Not the Houston clock. No locked day-count yet. | A full ship to the Gulf, then farther. Or a smaller ship. |

The East Coast pump is also fed by the Colonial pipeline from the Gulf, which is days, and by cargoes from Europe, which is a shorter ocean. A Hormuz barrel missed in Houston shows up in New England only after the Gulf plant and that pipeline. It does not show up in Los Angeles on that same day. Los Angeles was waiting on its own ship.

The barrels that had already sailed still arrive. The New Corolla did. The hole is the ship that never loaded. The screen should show a ship marker on each route, and the price moving on day one, before any marker lands.

Alaska to California is its own clock, Valdez to the Bay or to Los Angeles, and it does not pass Hormuz. The Kern pipe does not pass Hormuz either. Turning the strait off does not empty those two arrows.

## Oil and fat

They meet at the diesel pump and nowhere else. Fat does not make gasoline.

A crude barrel goes through a refinery. A fat barrel, soybean oil or tallow or used cooking oil, goes through a different unit and comes out as diesel or jet. The fat gallon costs more than the crude gallon unless a credit makes up the difference. The credits are a renewable-fuel credit and, in California, a low-carbon credit. Those are prices, when we have the series. They are not a reason to believe fat is cheap oil.

There is nothing to decide about which one is "really" diesel. Both are. The model shows the crude barrels and the fat barrels as separate supplies. If the credit series never shows up, the fat lever stays capped at the barrels that were made, and the credit stays a sentence. Not knowing which one "should" win is the right state. The pump total is the sum. The pie is not.

## Storage is the quiet gap

The gap is the later-month future minus today's spot.

Assume the gap is storage plus speculation. Storage is the cost of holding the barrel until the month arrives: the tank, the insurance, and the interest on the money. Trade desks put the Cushing tank at roughly 30 to 60 cents a barrel per month before interest. That is the size of a constant. It is not ten dollars.

Storage is a constant cents per barrel per month. It is not one number for every contract. A barrel held six months pays about six times the monthly constant.

The constant is the median monthly gap in the quiet weeks of the fit window, the weeks through June 16, 2025 when stocks were not full and there was no war. Speculation that week is the actual gap minus the months times that constant. It may be negative. Negative means the market wants the barrel now, more than storage can explain.

The constant is not a law. When the tanks are full, storage stops being a rent and becomes "nowhere to put it." April 2020 is that week. The front contract went below zero. Those weeks are marked no-room. They are not used to set the constant, and they are not called speculation.

The split is shown beside the retail miss. It is not subtracted from the miss to force the 1 percent.
