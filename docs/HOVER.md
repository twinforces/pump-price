# Hover copy

Standing rule: anything the user can point at that is a short name, or a price name, has an explanation. Hover on a pointer. Tap on a phone. Same text.

The sentence itself does not rely on an unexplained short name. These sims teach the author too.

The copy lives with the Model. A missing string is a test failure. These are mechanisms, not fitted coefficients.

## Prices

| Label | Copy |
| --- | --- |
| Gasoline | Street price of regular gasoline, including taxes. Same barrel as diesel, different cut. |
| Diesel | Street price of highway diesel, including taxes. Trucks, farms, and a lot of food move on this fuel. |
| Hamburger | Cows use a lot of diesel. Feed, cattle, and the meat all move by truck. The grocery price can lag the fuel price. The series is the Labor Department's average price for 100 percent ground beef, per pound, not a patty. |
| Bread | Wheat moves by rail and truck. Milling and baking use energy. Closer to gasoline and electricity than to the diesel crack. Series is the Labor Department's white pan bread, per pound. |
| Eggs | Hens eat corn and soy that arrive by truck. The birds themselves barely move. Fuel is not the only shock: avian flu has moved this price by itself. Series is the Labor Department's grade A large, per dozen. |
| Milk | Perishable, so it rides refrigerated trucks a short way. Where the Agriculture Department surveys a city in this state, that city's gallon is the price, and the hover names the city. Otherwise this is the Census region, and the hover names the region. |
| Toilet paper | Mostly electricity, not diesel. Display name only. The series is the Labor Department's household paper products index (tissue, towels, napkins), national, not a state price. |

The planning chat also said paper mills burn natural gas. The hover follows "mostly electricity" and does not pretend we split the mill's fuel bill.

## Names people will meet

| Label | Copy |
| --- | --- |
| Fuel district | One of five regions drawn in World War II to allocate gasoline and diesel. The Energy Information Administration still prices fuel this way. Not a Census region. Kentucky is fuel-Midwest and grocery-South. |
| State Energy Data System | The Energy Information Administration's annual energy book for every state. The gasoline figure is a yearly estimate in dollars per million British thermal units, a heat unit, not the weekly price on the sign. Federal and state tax are in. Local tax is out. Back to 1970. |
| Energy Information Administration | The federal energy statistics shop. Its weekly survey prints every state only for some fuels. |
| American Automobile Association | A station survey with gasoline and diesel for all 50 states. A different sample from the federal one. |
| GasBuddy | Crowd-sourced station prices. The public charts hold about 10 years of gasoline, not a file you can download. We use the gap versus the federal district, and we say so. |
| Labor Department | Publishes the grocery price levels. Food dollar prices stop at the four Census regions, except where another survey (milk, by city) is better. |
| Census region | Four fixed blocks: Northeast, Midwest, South, West. Huge. Not a shipping zone, and not a fuel district. |
| Bureau of Economic Analysis | Publishes an annual index of how expensive each state is. It is not a gallon of milk. |

## The annotation sentence

When a state fuel price is not itself a federal state series:

> Wisconsin gasoline: weekly shape from the Energy Information Administration's Midwest fuel district. Level shifted by the GasBuddy gap between Wisconsin and that Midwest series.

The donor is the fuel district that contains the state. Colorado is the Rocky Mountain district. It is the wrong donor for Wisconsin. Minnesota is inside the Midwest fuel district, so Minnesota is a legal state donor.

If the federal survey already prints the state, the hover says that, and GasBuddy is a check, not the spine.
