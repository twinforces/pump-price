# Runs, season, and the crude they buy

Locked 2026-09-27. Three corrections. Plants do not run flat out by default. The season is mostly demand, not a new recipe. The crude they buy moves the barrel inside a range the hardware already set.

## They do cut runs

The incentive is the crack. If the next barrel does not pay for itself, or the tanks are full, running less is the point. April 2020 is the proof. Weekly utilization fell to 67.6 percent of capacity. The monthly series, which starts in 1985, hit 70 percent, the low. Product demand that month was the lowest since at least 1981. Marathon idled Gallup and Martinez rather than make fuel nobody was buying.

A planned turnaround is the other cut. The plant is offline on purpose so it does not break, usually when demand is softer. Nameplate is not a promise.

When the crack is wide they push the hardware. That is a result, not a standing assumption. Utilization is a measured series. The fit reads it. It does not replace it with 100 percent.

## The season

Heating oil is the winter use of the distillate cut, mostly in the Northeast. The federal heating-oil price is collected only from October through March. Gasoline demand peaks with summer driving. Diesel for trucks does not have that winter spike.

Refiners do shift the barrel, and they change the summer gasoline recipe (it has to evaporate less in the heat). The national yield does not swing by much. In the second half of 2025, finished gasoline was about 45 percent of the barrel in July and about 47 percent in December. Distillate stayed near 30 percent the whole way. A couple of points, not a plant that becomes a heating-oil plant in November.

What moves with the weather is demand and stocks. Distillate stocks draw when the furnaces are on. The 2019 same-week baseline already carries that calendar. A separate winter recipe is not added on top unless a later fit shows the couple of points actually matter.

## Crude in, products out

Half right. The kit decides which crude they can buy. A coker can eat heavy oil. A simple plant cannot. Inside that kit, a heavier, sourer barrel leaves more residue, more coke, and usually more diesel, and less of the light cut that becomes gasoline. Marathon already prints the sour share of crude by region. That is the slate we can see. It is sweet versus sour, not the six quality slices.

The crude is not destiny. The same barrel can be cut a few points toward whichever product is paying, which is the season and the crack from the section above. Output is hardware, then slate, then that small choice. It is not slate alone.
