# Supply into the harbor

Locked 2026-09-27. The harbor-to-pump path sits on one constant per fuel. Supply into the harbor does not. No cents-per-day coefficient is fitted.

Days on hand are stocks divided by the day's use. The crack is the Gulf harbor minus crude divided by 42. Spring 2020 is out. 528 weeks, January 2015 through June 2025.

Diesel harbor over crude, by days of distillate on hand:

| Days on hand | Weeks | Typical crack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 25 to 30 | 123 | 60 cents |
| 30 to 35 | 198 | 51 cents |
| 35 to 40 | 123 | 37 cents |
| 40 to 50 | 70 | 31 cents |

Gasoline moves less, because the gasoline pile itself moves less. About 49 cents over crude when 20 to 23 days are on hand, and about 28 cents when 28 to 32 days are.

Refinery use, once the plants are above about 85 percent, does not lift the typical diesel crack. It stays near 50 cents from 85 percent up through a full plant. The weeks under 80 percent, mostly the demand collapse, sit near 22 cents.

The national pile is not the 2022 spike. April 29, 2022 had 27 days of diesel on hand and 88 percent of the plants running. The diesel harbor was about $2.30 over crude. The tight-pile bucket, 25 to 30 days, typically sits at 60 cents. Same pile, not the same harbor.
