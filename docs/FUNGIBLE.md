# The barrel can leave

Locked 2026-09-27. A domestic pile is not the demand. Oil is fungible, and so is diesel. The Gulf harbor is a world price. No foreign-price coefficient is fitted. The foreign harbor itself is not in the score yet.

Distillate exports, spring 2020 out, against the Gulf diesel harbor minus crude divided by 42:

| Exports, million barrels a day | Weeks | Typical crack |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.9 to 1.2 | 230 | 48 cents |
| 1.2 to 1.5 | 218 | 48 cents |
| 1.5 to 1.8 | 39 | 51 cents |

2022 exported a typical 1.3 million barrels a day of distillate. Other years exported about 1.2. The 2022 crack was typically $1.26. Other years, 46 cents. On April 29, 2022, exports were 1.2 million barrels a day and the crack was about $2.30. The quantity did not jump. The price did. That is what fungible means: the outside bid sets the harbor even when the barrels that leave look ordinary.

Crude did not stop crossing the border. In 2022 about 6.2 million barrels a day came in and about 3.6 million left. Across the whole window the country remained a net importer of crude.

The self-sufficiency claim is about light oil. Most of what this country produces is light sweet crude. Many of the plants were built to run heavy sour crude, because that barrel is cheaper and those plants already paid for the equipment that can eat it. The light barrel is the one that gets exported. The heavy barrel is the one that still comes in.
