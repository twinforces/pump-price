# Crude quality

Locked 2026-09-27. First of the open blanks. The others stay open.

## The Factbook does not have this

The Central Intelligence Agency country page no longer returns a petroleum table. What comes back is a notice that the World Factbook has been retired. Even when it was alive it counted barrels, reserves, and trade. It did not print gravity or sulfur. It cannot fill this table.

## The cut we will use

The Energy Information Administration's crude-import tool, in a February 10, 2015 note, is the federal source that uses the words light, heavy, sweet, and sour together.

| Word | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Light | API gravity 35 or above |
| Heavy | API gravity 27 or below |
| Medium | In between |
| Sweet | Sulfur 0.5 percent or less, by weight |
| Sour | Sulfur above 0.5 percent |

API gravity runs backwards. Water is 10. A higher number is a lighter oil.

A different federal page, on refinery crude quality, says light crudes generally exceed 38 and heavy crudes are commonly 22 or below. A 2019 workshop slide at the same agency used light above 33 and heavy below 25, with the same 0.5 percent sulfur line. We do not average these. The import-tool line is the one that matches the words on the levers. The other two stay as a conflict, so a barrel on the boundary is marked as a boundary and not quietly moved.

## Four corners were not enough

Medium sour is most of the oil the levers will turn off. Saudi Arab Light, Iraqi Basrah, Iranian export grades, Kuwait, Dubai, Oman, and Russian Urals all land in the middle on gravity and on the sour side of 0.5 percent sulfur. Forcing them into "heavy sour" or "light sweet" to save a four-slice pie would be the false picture.

The pie has six slices: light, medium, and heavy, each sweet or sour. A country is not a slice. A country is a list of streams. Turning the country off zeroes every stream on its list.

## What this does to the lever

Turning off Saudi Arabia is not turning off heavy sour. It is turning off a stack that is mostly medium sour, plus a heavy grade that sits on the 27-degree line.

Turning off the United States is not turning off light sweet either. The shale barrels are light sweet. Mars and Alaska are medium sour. The lever has to say which.

Canada's export that moves the heavy-sour slice is Western Canadian Select. Mexico's is Maya. Iraq's heavy grade is Basrah Heavy. Those three are the clean heavy-sour switches.

Nigeria's famous light grade is sweet and a hair too heavy for the light line. It stays medium sweet until an assay puts it at 35 or above.
