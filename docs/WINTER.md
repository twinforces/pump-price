# Winter

Locked 2026-09-27. Winter diesel costs more than gasoline at the pump. That premium is already in the diesel harbor. It is the furnace bidding for the same part of the barrel the truck burns.

Heating oil is No. 2 distillate, the same cut as on-road diesel, now at the same ultra-low sulfur specification. The difference that matters at the tax window is dye and the federal tax, not a different molecule. The furnaces are mostly in the Northeast. They burn it in winter and almost not at all in July. The New York harbor quote that the trade still calls heating oil is the benchmark for both the furnace and the truck.

The Gulf feels it because the Gulf is where a lot of that barrel is made, and the pipeline and the ships carry it north. That is why the Gulf pump, not just the East Coast pump, shows diesel ahead of gasoline in January.

January 2015 through the week of June 16, 2025. March through June 2020 left out. The extra 6 cents of federal diesel tax is taken out of the pump comparison, so the gap is not the tax.

Diesel pump minus gasoline pump, January minus July:

| | January minus July |
| --- | --- |
| Gulf | 22 cents |
| Midwest | 27 cents |
| East Coast | 16 cents |

Gulf diesel over its own harbor, after federal tax and 20 cents of Texas tax, is about 54 cents in January and about 55 cents in July. January minus July on that leftover is about 4 cents. Gasoline's leftover is a few cents higher in summer, not winter.

If the winter cost were a recipe added at the rack, on top of the harbor, the leftover would rise in January. It does not. The furnace moved the harbor. Nothing is added again on the way to the sign.
