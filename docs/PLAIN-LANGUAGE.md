# Plain language

Standing rule, tightened: the sim teaches the author too. A user-facing string does not use a short name unless that same string says what it means. Hover still carries the longer version.

## Fuel districts

Petroleum Administration for Defense Districts. Five regions drawn in World War II so the government could allocate gasoline and diesel. The Energy Information Administration still publishes fuel prices on those lines. They are not Census regions, and they are not states.

Kentucky, Oklahoma, and Tennessee are in the fuel "Midwest" and in the grocery "South." That mismatch is the reason both names exist.

The weekly price tables use these pieces. A finer refining map cuts New York and Texas into county pieces. For a pump price, we follow the weekly table and keep the state whole.

| District | What the price table calls it | States |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1A | New England | Connecticut, Maine, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Rhode Island, Vermont |
| 1B | Central Atlantic | Delaware, Maryland, New Jersey, New York, Pennsylvania, and the District of Columbia |
| 1C | Lower Atlantic | Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, South Carolina, Virginia, West Virginia |
| 2 | Midwest | Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska, North Dakota, Ohio, Oklahoma, South Dakota, Tennessee, Wisconsin |
| 3 | Gulf Coast | Alabama, Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, New Mexico, Texas |
| 4 | Rocky Mountain | Colorado, Idaho, Montana, Utah, Wyoming |
| 5 | West Coast | Alaska, Arizona, California, Hawaii, Nevada, Oregon, Washington |

District 1 with no letter is the whole East Coast, the three pieces above added together.

Source for the origin and the state lists: the Wikipedia page "Petroleum Administration for Defense Districts," checked against the Energy Information Administration's Petroleum Supply Monthly appendix of district descriptions (August 2026). Where they disagree, the weekly price table wins for prices, and the appendix wins for a refinery's county.

## State Energy Data System

The Energy Information Administration's annual book of energy for every state: how much was used, what it cost, what was spent. Not a weekly pump survey.

The gasoline entry is an estimate of the average price for the year. It is stored as dollars per million British thermal units, a heat unit, not a gallon. Federal and state tax are in. Local tax is out. The file code is MGACD: motor gasoline, price, transportation. It runs back to 1970. You can turn it into an approximate dollar-per-gallon figure. It is a check on the yearly shape, not the number on the sign Friday.

A true monthly pump price for every state, excluding taxes, existed from 1983 through 2011. Collection then stopped. Do not extend it.

The same system also tracks distillate fuel oil, which includes diesel and heating oil. That is not the on-highway diesel price on our map.

Source: Energy Information Administration frequently asked question 26, and the State Energy Data System documentation guide.

## Is there a grocery GasBuddy?

Not one that covers hamburger, bread, eggs, milk, and toilet paper, for all 50 states, as a history.

| Source | What it really is | Use? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labor Department average prices | Dollar prices for specific foods. National, and the four Census regions when the sample is big enough. Not by state. | Spine for hamburger, bread, and eggs. |
| What's the Grocery Bill | A site that says state food prices come from the Labor Department. The Labor Department does not publish those state series. | No, until their method is shown. |
| Agriculture Department Retail Milk Prices | Monthly gallons of conventional whole milk in selected cities. Three stores, one day early in the month, most common brand, not a sale price. September 2026 simple average was $3.97 a gallon. Cities in the same state can differ. | Yes for milk, where the state has a surveyed city. Name the city. Other states stay on the Census region. |
| Economic Analysis regional price parities | An annual index of how expensive a state is versus the country. 100 means the national level. Goods are separate from housing, and goods are not "groceries only." | Not a milk price. A later nudge, only if the hover says it is all goods. |
| Numbeo | People typing city prices. Milk per liter, bread per 500 grams, eggs per dozen, beef per kilogram. Not our package sizes, not a sample survey, not every state. | Only as a labeled cross-check. Not the history. |
| Self.inc grocery article | One Walmart.com price near one ZIP code per state, one moment. | A snapshot, not a history. |
| Food-at-Home Monthly Area Prices | Scanner data, 15 areas, 2012 through 2018. | Too old and too coarse to be the live map. |

Toilet paper still has no state or city dollar series in hand. The national household-paper index remains the series, and the hover says so.
